Possessive Adjectives

Les adjectifs possessifs · A1
mon · ma · meston · ta · tesson · sa · sesnotre · nosvotre · vosleur · leurs

Do not translate possessive adjectives directly from English — French uses a different rule. Let's learn the French way!

A possessive adjective shows who owns something — my, your, his, her… In English, the possessive adjective matches the owner. In French, the possessive adjective matches the noun (the thing that is owned). This lesson shows you how to choose the proper French form.

The big difference

English → the owner

Eric looks at his car.

Liz looks at her car.

his / her because the owner is different.

French → the noun

Eric regarde sa voiture.

Liz regarde sa voiture.

both sa — voiture is feminine. The owner doesn't matter.

In French, look at the NOUN, not the owner.

How to choose — 2 steps
1

Who owns it?

Choose the owner's set: je → mon / ma / mes · tu → ton / ta / tes · il / elle / on → son / sa / ses.

2

What is owned?

Look at the noun — is the gender masculine or feminine? Is the number singular or plural, and does the noun start with a vowel?

The vowel-sound rule
Before a vowel or a silent h, use mon, ton, son — even with a feminine noun. The noun stays feminine.
