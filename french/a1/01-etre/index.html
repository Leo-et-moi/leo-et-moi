Comment prononcer le verbe Être ?
Couleurs : liaison · lettre muette · accent
Pronunciation / La liaison "z" : in French you pronounce the liaison between the consonant "s" and the following vowel by making a "z" sound. Example: je suis‿américain
Pronunciation / Silent letters: in French the final consonants D, P, S, T, X and Z are generally not pronounced (silent letters), BUT the final consonants C, R, F and L are. There are exceptions — see the pronunciation lessons: the alphabet, accents and liaisons. Ex: je suis, étudiant
Pronunciation / Accent aigu: in French an acute accent (é) on an "e" gives it a closed "ay" sound. It usually appears at the beginning or end of a syllable. Ex: étudiant, fatigué
Identité : Nationalité / Profession / Rôle
Je suis‿américain
I am American
Je suis‿étudiant
I am a student
Status : État / Description
Je suis fatigué
I am tired
Il est réparé
It is fixed
Pronunciation Tip! Repeat: je suis‿[zaméricain] – je suis‿[zétudiant] = liaison "z"
Pronunciation Tip! Repeat: je suis fatigué – je [sui] fatigué = "s" is a silent letter

Écran 5 / 12
TU ES — You are (informal)
Couleurs : liaison · lettre muette · accent
Tu es
you are
Note: a statement said with a rising voice is a question! "Tu es‿américain ?" ↗
Tu es‿américain ?
Are you American?
Tu es de Portland ?
Are you from Portland?
Tu es fatigué ?
Are you tired?
Le français distingue le TUTOIEMENT (tu) et le VOUVOIEMENT (vous), alors que l'anglais n'utilise qu'un seul pronom (you).
Utilise « tu » avec tes amis, tes camarades de classe, ta famille et les personnes que tu connais bien.
French distinguishes between the informal form (tu) and the formal form (vous), whereas English uses only one pronoun (you).
Use "tu" with friends, classmates, family, people you know well.

Écran 7 / 12
NOUS SOMMES — We are (formal)
Couleurs : liaison · lettre muette · accent
Nous sommes
We are (formal)
Nous sommes‿américains
We are American
Nous sommes‿amis
We are friends
Nous sommes de l'Oregon
We are from Oregon
"Nous sommes" is the formal written form. In speech, French people say "On‿est" instead. In writing (essays, letters) use NOUS. In conversation use ON. Both mean "we".
Écran 8 / 12
VOUS ÊTES — You are (formal / plural)
Couleurs : liaison · lettre muette · accent
"Vous" has two meanings: (1) one person, spoken formally / politely; (2) several people at once. Example: speaking to your teacher (formal singular), or to a whole class (plural).
Vous‿êtes
You are (formal / plural)
Vous‿êtes professeur !
You are a teacher! (formal)
Reminder: a statement said with a rising voice is a question! "Vous‿êtes prêts ?" ↗
Vous‿êtes de quelle ville ?
What city are you from?
Vous‿êtes prêts ?
Are you all ready? (plural)
Écran 9 / 12
ILS SONT / ELLES SONT — They are
Couleurs : liaison · lettre muette · accent
Ils sont  ·  Elles sont
They are
Ils sont sportifs
They are athletic
Leo et son‿ami sont sportifs
Leo and his friend are athletic
Le masculin l'emporte sur le féminin — the masculine form takes precedence whenever both genders are present in the same group. Ex: Leo et sa sœur Cocoa sont‿américains. You don't say: …américaines.
Leo et sa sœur Cocoa sont‿américains
Leo and his sister are Americans
Marlowe et sa sœur Cocoa sont‿américaines
Marlowe and her sister Cocoa are American
L'accord de genre et de nombre — words in a sentence agree by gender (masculine / feminine) and number (singular / plural). Ex: Elles sont‿étudiantes. You don't say: Elles sont‿étudiant.
Ils sont de Paris
They are from Paris
Elles sont‿heureuses
They are happy
Elles sont‿étudiantes
They are students
Pronunciation / La liaison "t" : in French you pronounce the liaison between the consonant "t" and the following vowel by making a "t" sound. Example: elles sont‿heureuses. Note: the liaison is made with the "e", not the "h", because "h" is a silent letter.
Pronunciation / Le h muet : in French you don't pronounce the consonant "h". Unlike in English, French never pronounces the letter "h".
Pronunciation Tip! Repeat: hôpital – [opital], hôtel – [otel] = silent "h"

Key rules
1. Être is irregular — memorize all 6 forms.
2. Être + adjective: the adjective agrees in gender and number with the subject.
3. "On" replaces "nous" in everyday spoken French.
4. Watch your pronunciation: liaisons (link words), silent final letters, and accents (é = "ay").
