Écran 6 / 13
TU ES — You are (informal)
Tu es
you are
Tu es‿américain ?
Are you American?
Tu es de Portland ?
Are you from Portland?
Tu es fatigué ?
Are you tired?
Le français distingue le TUTOIEMENT (tu) et le VOUVOIEMENT (vous), alors que l'anglais n'utilise qu'un seul pronom (you). Utilise « tu » avec tes amis, tes camarades de classe, ta famille et les personnes que tu connais bien.
French distinguishes between the informal form (tu) and the formal form (vous), whereas English uses only one pronoun (you). Use "tu" with friends, classmates, family, people you know well.
Note: a statement said with a rising voice is a question! "Tu es‿américain ?" ↗

"On" is used instead of "nous" in everyday spoken French. "On est" = « Nous sommes » (We are).
Remember: Il = he (masculine) • Elle = she (feminine) • On = one / we (informal).

NOUS SOMMES — We are (formal)
Nous sommes
We are (formal)
Nous sommes‿américains
We are American
Nous sommes‿amis
We are friends
Nous sommes de l'Oregon
We are from Oregon
"Nous sommes" is the formal written form. In speech, French people say "On est" instead. In writing (essays, letters) use NOUS. In conversation use ON. Both mean "we".
Écran 9 / 13
VOUS ÊTES — You are (formal / plural)
Vous‿êtes
You are (formal / plural)
Vous‿êtes professeur !
You are a teacher! (formal)
Vous‿êtes de quelle ville ?
What city are you from?
Vous‿êtes prêts ?
Are you all ready? (plural)
Reminder: a statement said with a rising voice is a question! "Vous‿êtes prêts ?" ↗
"Vous" has two meanings: (1) one person, spoken formally / politely; (2) several people at once. Example: speaking to your teacher (formal singular), or to a whole class (plural).
Écran 10 / 13
ILS SONT / ELLES SONT — They are
Ils sont  ·  Elles sont
They are
Ils sont sportifs
They are athletic
Ils sont de Paris
They are from Paris
Elles sont‿heureuses
They are happy
Elles sont‿étudiantes
They are students
Gender agreement: if even ONE person in the group is masculine, use ILS. Use ELLES only when all the people in the group are feminine.
Leo (un garçon) + Marie (une fille) = ILS sont… • Sophie + Emma (deux filles) = ELLES sont…
La liaison : with "sont", link the "t" onto the next word: sont‿heureuses (the h is silent — French never pronounces the letter h).
Pronunciation tip: elles sont‿heureuses → [t] carry the "t" sound over. Listen: Elles sont‿heureuses

"Accord de genre et de nombre" with the verb "to be": add an "e" if the subject is feminine — he is tired (masculine), she is tired (feminine). Add a plural "s" if the subject is plural — Nous sommes malades.

Key rules
1. Être is irregular — memorize all 6 forms.
2. Être + adjective: the adjective agrees in gender (and number) with the subject.
3. "On" replaces "nous" in everyday spoken French.
